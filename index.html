The codebook

noki

A secret language for two. It has very few letters, very few words, and the words never change. Learn it here, then use it anywhere. Nobody else will understand.

  • 15 letters
  • 48 words
  • 2 question words
  • 0 word endings to learn

    Lesson 1 of 8

    Say it right

    Noki has only 15 letters, and each one always sounds the same.

    aeiou dfkalways hardlmnpsalways stvas in very

    Four sound rules

    1. Vowels sound like Spanish: a, e, i, o, u. Say them fully, never a lazy “uh”.

    2. The first part of a word is the strong part: LE-fu, SU-li, KI-fi.

    3. v sounds like the English v in “very”: soft, top teeth on your bottom lip. Never like a b. The hear buttons say it like a b: ignore them and say v.

    4. kui is one quick sound, like the “cui” in cuidado.

    The hear buttons use your device's Spanish voice. If a word sounds odd, trust these rules.

    Short words are the glue. Words like ka, si and e have one part. The words for things and actions have two parts, like fo-ma.

    Secret detail: vek (no) is the only word that ends with a hard sound, so nobody ever misses a “no”.

    Lesson 2 of 8

    Who

    Three tiny words for people, plus yes and no — and how to stick them together.

    lo means he or she. Noki doesn't care which.

    Two people? Say both. si ka = we (you and me). lo ka = we, but not you.

    Same one twice? That's more of them. si si = you two, or all of you. lo lo = they.

    That lets Noki say something English can't say in one word: si ka lefu = you and I are going. lo ka lefu = he and I are going, you're not.

    The one you never say is ka ka — it means something else in Spanish! Say si ka or lo ka.

    Lesson 3 of 8

    Things

    Words for stuff, and how to say whose it is.

    Some things are also actions. foma is food and eat. vimo is water and drink. fipo is play, a game, and fun.

    Whose is it? Put the person first: ka tuka = my home.

    That one: put ke first: ke suno = that person.

    No Noki word for it? Just say the normal word. Names never change. Tip: use the language the people around you understand less.

    If you keep borrowing the same word, put it on your wishlist (on paper or in your phone's notes). It might become a Noki word one day.

    Lesson 4 of 8

    Doing

    Actions, and your first real sentences.

    Who, then action, then what: ka suli vimo = I want water.

    No “who”? Then it's an order: lefu tuka! = Go home!

    Two actions together: the second one is what you want or need to do. ka suli lefu tuka = I want to go home.

    A person after an action is who you mean it for: dafu ka! = help me! · milu ka! = come to me! · ka lefu si = I'll go with you. If there's a thing too, the person comes first: moku ka vimo = give me water.

    Actions can be things, too. Where a word sits decides its job: ka dafu si = I help you, ka duni dafu:what = I need help.

    Lesson 5 of 8

    Not

    One word turns anything around.

    Before an action, vek means not: ka vek naki = I don't understand.

    Before a thing, it means none: vek suno = nobody.

    With no “who”, it means don't: vek doki! = Don't tell!

    Lesson 6 of 8

    When

    Before, now, later. That's the whole time system.

    Words never change. To say when, put a time word right before the action.

    panu = it already happened. kifi = right now. lati = it will happen.

    No time word? Then you'll know from what's going on.

    A time word only works on the next action. Use e (and) to add another action with its own time word.

    Tip: ka foma can mean “my food” or “I eat”. Add a time word to make it an action: ka kifi foma:do = I'm eating now.

    No action? The time word still goes in the same place. That's how you say there is, there was and there will be: vimo lati meni = there will be water here. It also gives you was and will be: ka panu mufo:desc = I was tired.

    Lesson 7 of 8

    Where, and questions

    Here, there, the two question words, and “if”.

    Places go at the end: si ka lefu lopa = we're going there.

    Yes-or-no questions: just say it like a question. si naki? = do you understand?

    A question with no time word also means “shall we” or “can we”: si ka lefu? = shall we go? · si ka kifi deli? = can we leave now? That's why Noki needs no word for “can”.

    Every other question uses kui. Put kui where the answer would go. The answer goes in the same place.

    AskMeansAnswer
    si suli kui?What do you want?ka suli vimo.
    kui milu?Who is coming?lo milu.
    si lefu kui?Where are you going?ka lefu tuka.
    si kui lefu?When are you going?ka lati lefu.

    Just kui? on its own means “What?” or “Huh?”

    Why?

    kui can't ask why, so Noki has one more small word: fa. It works like e (and), but what comes after it is the reason. On its own, fa? means “Why?”

    If…, then…

    Noki has no word for “if”. Say the “if” part first, like a question, then what happens. lati (later) works as “then”. We already talk like this: “Finished your homework? Then you can play.”

    The “if” part always comes first. If it sounds like a real question, what you say next makes it clear.

    Lesson 8 of 8

    Good, bad, and more

    Describe things and how you feel, and say it twice to make it bigger.

    No word for “is”: ke suno pomi = that person is good. ka mufo:desc = I'm tired.

    Say it twice to make it more. pomi pomi = very good! ka mela mela si = I love you so much.

    For things, count by saying it again: suno = one person, suno suno = two people, suno suno suno = lots of people. How many? tolu? — suno tolu? = how many people?

    And on people: si si = you all, lo lo = they.

    It works on time words and actions too: panu panu = long ago, lati lati = much later or another day, kifi kifi = right now!, fipo fipo! = play again!

    How do you feel?

    Happy? Just say ka pomi. A lie? ke vek koti = that's not true.
